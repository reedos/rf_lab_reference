Two-tone envelope, IMD3 / IP3, P1dB, and THD. Power is per tone at the DUT plane unless noted.

Per-tone powerper port
dBm

Two equal CW tones add in voltage at envelope peaks. Peak envelope power is +6.02 dB vs one tone — the DUT sees twice the CW VOPP you would calculate from a single tone at that dBm. That is why a two-tone IMD sweep compresses sooner than a CW P1dB sweep at the same per-tone power.

IIP3 = Ptone + Δ/2, where Δ is the gap from each tone to IM3. OIP3 = IIP3 + G. Rule of thumb for a cubic nonlinearity: OP1dB ≈ OIP3 − 10 dB. +1 dB on both tones → +3 dB on IM3.

THD = √(Σ 10Hn/10) with Hn in dBc. A single −40 dBc harmonic is 1% THD. Harmonic dBm = Pfund + dBc. Receiver harmonics on a VNA can fake DUT THD — check with a notch or a spectrum analyzer when it matters.

Rules of thumb

Order-of-magnitude only

Two equal tones PEP = Ptone + 6 dB Envelope Vpp is 2× the CW VOPP at that per-tone dBm
IMD3 vs P1dB OIP3 ≈ OP1dB + 10 dB Cubic class-A estimate; real parts wander several dB
Tone step +1 dB tones → +3 dB IM3 3rd-order: ΔIM3 = 3 ΔPin. If it is not ~3:1, you are not in the cubic region
